      How to play

      Play in threes. Each round, one person brings the news, one plays a character and one referees. Swap after every round so everyone plays all three roles.

      The roles

      Newsbearer

      Draw a news story as your prompt. Talk to the character in a personal, relatable way, using the three steps below. You can stick to your own personal story, or take the extra challenge of weaving the news in. You win the round if the character says yes to your one thing and you get no red card.

      Character

      Draw a character and read out the front of the card: name, age, job and scores. Show the triggers and door openers to the referee only. Start the round with your opening line, then stay in role: open up when the newsbearer finds a door opener and push back when they hit a trigger.

      Referee

      Read the character's triggers and door openers. Run the five-minute clock, hold up cards when the newsbearer slips, and call the verdict at the end.

      The story and the character are drawn separately, at random.

      The conversation, in order

      1. My why

        Open with why you care, through a moment from your own life. If you are using the news story, connect the two. A few sentences is enough.

        Based on Simon Sinek, Start with Why

      2. Your world

        Ask about the character's life and listen for what they feel and what they need. Show them you heard it.

        Based on Marshall Rosenberg, Nonviolent Communication

      3. One thing

        Ask for one action. It has to pass the detective test.

        Motive
        They care about it, for their own reasons.
        Means
        They have the money, time and skills.
        Opportunity
        They could do it this week.

        Detectives use motive, means and opportunity to work out who could have committed a crime. Here you're planning a small act of climate action, and the character is your accomplice.

      Cards

      Yellow card: a warning

      The newsbearer keeps going and drops whatever earned the card.

      • A statistic or number the character didn't ask for
      • Jargon or acronyms, such as net zero, mitigation, decarbonise, carbon footprint, COP or IPCC
      • Talking for 30 seconds without asking a question
      • Telling them what to do: "you should", "you need to"
      • Doom: "it's too late", "we're all doomed"

      Red card: stop and repair

      The newsbearer stops the story and repairs the conversation before going on.

      • A second yellow card
      • Hitting one of the character's triggers
      • Blaming, mocking or shaming the character
      • Arguing with how they feel

      Repair after a red card

      Say what you think the character feels and needs, then check you got it right.

      "It sounds like you're [feeling] because [need] matters to you. Is that right?"

      Wait for the answer. If they say no, try again. Carry on once they agree you've understood.

      End of the round

      • The character answers: would you do it? Yes, maybe or no. If it isn't a yes, they say which was missing: motive, means or opportunity.
      • The referee names one moment that worked and one to replay.

      Debrief questions

      1. Which one thing would you actually do this week?
      2. What earned a red card, and what repaired it?
      3. Who in your own life is most like your character? What will you say to them now?
